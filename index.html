How it works

The forecast tells you how many patients are coming. The software shows what that does to the rest of the hospital, and what to do about it.

1

Model it

We map how your ER, labs, imaging, inpatient units, OR, and cleaning crews depend on each other, using data you already have.

2

Find the cause

Your team uses our software to see where delays start, not just where they show up, and where they’ll move next.

3

Test every fix

Try a hire, a shift change, a scanner, or a new schedule before paying for it. Every option runs on the same week with the same patients, with its cost and yearly net gain side by side.

Just a few of the hundreds of questions we answer

Each of these is a decision a hospital makes a few times a year, usually from a dashboard and a consultant's report.

Do we need a second CT scanner, or a different scan schedule?

The twin runs both on the same week. In the demo hospital, moving scheduled scans out of the ER peak does more than two hires for a tenth of the cost.

Will another hospitalist fix boarding?

Faster admission orders do nothing when the beds do not open until afternoon. The twin shows which part of the chain is actually holding the patient.

Staff nurses, travel nurses, or only on the days we need them?

The forecast says which days the beds overflow. Staffing those days alone can give the best return per dollar.

What does the new physician shift actually buy us?

Sometimes minutes. When rooms and imaging are the constraint, a provider is the most expensive fix that changes the least.

Built on a forecast we’ve already proven

To test any fix, you first need to know how many patients will show up. So we built that first: a forecast of daily ER patient volume, tested on 18 months of real ER visits.
